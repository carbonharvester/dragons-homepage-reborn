Child
Protection.

Our position on safeguarding, photo and naming consent, contact with vulnerable children, and what schools and parents should expect from us.

The headline: students never have direct one-on-one contact with vulnerable children at any partner school we work with. Our programmes are designed around the work — not around the kids. That is the structural choice that prevents most of the harm typical voluntourism creates.

The four non-negotiables

  • No direct one-on-one contact with vulnerable children. No 1:1 mentoring, no orphanage visits, no classroom-takeover sessions. Visiting students contribute to the work alongside the adult Kenyan crew or community — not to the children.
  • 1:10 leader-to-student ratio (minimum two leaders per trip). Every trip is led on the ground by Kenyan trip leaders we contract directly. The minimum holds regardless of cohort size.
  • Risk assessment per trip. Each itinerary is risk-assessed before departure. Health, safety, and safeguarding checks are run at every site we use.
  • Photo and naming consent. We aspire to publish identifiable photos of Kenyan children only with informed consent from a parent or guardian. We are working toward 100% verifiable consent on every image we publish; until that's audited and stable, we apply caution by default.

Adults featured by name

We feature Kenyan adults (partners, trip leaders, farm crew, community members) by name only with their consent. We name our partners — Elly Oenga, the team at Food for Education, the Wildlife Works conservancy partners — because they have agreed to be named.

What we do not do

  • Place students in unsupervised contact with children at partner schools.
  • Run "teach for a day" sessions where international students lead a Kenyan classroom.
  • Use children as the emotional climax of a trip film or marketing asset.
  • Publish "before / after" stories about identifiable children.
  • Bring journalists, filmmakers, or photographers without a signed media protocol agreed by the Kenyan partner.

Visiting cohort safeguarding

Every cohort flies with at least one designated safeguarding lead from the visiting school. Kapes Adventures does not replace the school's safeguarding role; we add to it. On the ground, our Kenyan trip leaders hold first-aid certification, child-protection training, and clear escalation protocols.

Reporting concerns

If you have a concern about how we handle child protection — on a trip, in a piece of communication, or in our partner relationships — contact the founder directly. We will investigate, document, and respond.
