    A Dubai-based, Kenyan-led educational-travel partner running three year-round programmes in Kenya — Seeds2Education, Water Empowering Women, Community Conservation. Visiting cohorts join an existing programme; they don't initiate one. We're not a trip company.

    Hunger keeps children out of class (S2E — onion farms feed pupils). Water scarcity sends girls to fetch (WEW — tanks at partner schools). Single-story narratives limit what students imagine (CC — conservancy + factory + bursaries on one site). Each runs whether a cohort visits or not.

Impact metrics published annually, not just Year-1 PR. Same safeguarding standard centrally underwritten — schools inherit it.

The most common parent + leadership concern on any Kenya trip is safety. This page documents the posture; the next logs individual risks + mitigations.

Trip lead, medic, driver, safeguarding officer — all resident in Kenya. 24/7 on call.
OPERATING POSTURE · THREE PILLARS
01 · Transparent
Parents see everything
Full risk register, evacuation plan, travel-advisory response protocol, and named medical-cover underwriter published to parents before registration opens — not after. Nothing in the operational plan is confidential to the provider.
02 · Layered
3 independent escalation paths
Student → school chaperone → Kapes trip lead → Kapes Adventures duty phone (Dubai) → Head of School. Independent medical contact via Flying Doctors Kenya direct line. No single-point-of-failure in the comms chain.
03 · Reversible
Any activity can be stood down mid-week
Any single activity can be cancelled without cascading the rest of the week. Weather, illness, or travel-advisory shift triggers the pre-signed alternate itinerary (Appendix C). The build days can compress into two if needed.
● SAFEGUARDING COMMIT
All Kapes ground staff hold current DBS-equivalent checks (Kenya DCI clearance). No 1:1 adult-child contact is permitted off-site or unsupervised. All photography of Kenyan children requires signed community consent; photography of visiting students requires parental opt-in collected at registration.

Q0 · Choosing
"How do schools in the group choose between the three programmes?"
Match by cohort fit. S2E — food security / sustainability / geography (Y10–Y11). WEW — gender equity / infrastructure / service learning (Y9–Y11). CC — Sixth Form ready for a more complex narrative challenge. Same standard, same delivery model, same fee across all three. Schools commonly rotate across years.
Q1 · Safety
"Is Kenya safe for 15-year-olds right now?"
Nairobi and the Rongai corridor are within standard FCDO "see our advice and prepare" guidance — the same risk tier as much of Southeast Asia. Our itinerary stays within that operating envelope. Coastal regions are excluded. Advisory monitored daily; trip auto-cancels on upgrade to "essential travel only". See p.10 for residuals.
Q2 · Voluntourism
"How is this not just voluntourism with a coat of paint?"
Three tests, all documented: (1) the programme runs year-round without us — six Kenyan staff on continuing payroll; (2) our students are assigned to Kenyan leads, not the reverse; (3) the infrastructure they help build stays in active service. We do not design projects for visitors. We run a programme; your cohort joins it mid-build.
Q3 · Cost
"USD 3,000 is a lot. What's actually different?"
USD 3,000 is the per-student fee for any of the three programmes — same pricing posture across S2E, WEW and CC. Leadership sees the total, the share going directly to the community build, and the non-negotiable safeguards it funds. 20% of the fee flows straight into the partner-school build (S2E onion farm / WEW tank / CC bursary fund), bonded by payment escrow. Benchmark providers charge USD 3,800–6,000 with no public equivalent. Flights are excluded and quoted separately at booking.
Q4 · Academics
"How is this tied to what we actually teach?"
Full IB CAS mapping on p.8 (all seven learning outcomes evidenced), optional DP Global Politics / Geography EE pathway, UAE National Agenda food-security pillar. Pre-trip modules sit in the existing Service Learning timetable slot. No new period pressure.
Q5 · Precedent
"Have other schools done this? Any references?"
The pilot cohort was a Dubai IB school's Grade 11 in Nov 2025 — written reference available. Two additional references on request: an IB school in Zurich (2024) and a UK independent (2023). Three Heads available to take a call before the senior team review if helpful.
Q6 · Optics
"What if a parent posts a photo that makes us look bad?"
Pre-trip seminar on savior framing is explicit and assessed. Social-media guidance issued to students + parents before departure. Photos of Kenyan children require community consent obtained on-site. We've built the programme to be photographable honestly — the problem doesn't arise if the work actually looks like partnership.
