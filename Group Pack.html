The short version,
for anyone who

  1. The programme
    Seeds2Education converts degraded school land into productive gardens and food forests. The produce feeds the children on-site. It is not a trip company. It is a year-round Kenyan-led food-security programme our students will join.
  2. The problem we're joining
    1 in 4 Kenyan children face stunted growth from malnutrition. Hunger is the #1 barrier to learning in the partner communities. Those same schools sit on unused land that could feed them.

    Three days of farmland conversion with Oloosirkon Primary School's staff and pupils. Onion-farming + agronomy workshops with the Kenyan team. Cultural exchange. A Food For Education facility visit. Not saviours — participants.

    The programme continues after the bus leaves — gardens keep feeding kids 11 months a year. BS 8848 conformant. LOTC Quality Badge. Pricing principles transparent. Impact metrics published annually, not just Year-1 PR.

Before the mechanism · the gap we're closing

Hungry children
can't learn.

This is the documented gap Seeds2Education is built to close. We lead with it because leadership should be able to name the problem before evaluating the solution.
1 in 4
Kenyan children under 5 with stunted growth from chronic malnutrition.
Kenya Demographic & Health Survey, 2022
#1
Cited cause of lost school attendance in partner communities.
Partner-school intake survey · Oloosirkon Primary School · 2024
70%
Of tourism revenue never reaches the local communities that host visitors.
UNCTAD tourism-leakage briefing, 2023
● What leadership should understand
This isn't about far-away statistics. In the specific partner schools we'll visit, teachers report children falling asleep in the afternoon because their last meal was the night before. Attendance thins after the long rains. The community knows the problem. The schools themselves own plots of land — degraded, disused — that could feed every child on their register, were the water, seeds and labour in place.
● Why visits alone don't fix it
The voluntourism critique is now well-documented: short visits that build one-off projects tend to dissolve when visitors leave. Painted libraries don't lift literacy. Solar panels installed by volunteers don't improve grades. A credible programme has to be continuous, community-led, and owned locally — with visitors joining it, not initiating it.
"Communities need more than tourists — they need partners."

The parent question
answered first.

The most common parent + leadership concern on any Kenya trip is safety. This page documents the posture; the next logs individual risks + mitigations.
● 01
BS 8848 conformant
British Standard for visits, fieldwork, expeditions and adventurous activities outside the UK.
● 02
LOTC Quality Badge
Learning Outside the Classroom accreditation · reviewed 2024 · next renewal Oct 2026.
● 03
£10m PL cover
Public liability underwritten by Endsleigh. Certificate on file, Appendix A.
● 04
Kenyan-led ground team
Trip lead, medic, driver, safeguarding officer — all resident in Kenya. 24/7 on call.
OPERATING POSTURE · THREE PILLARS
01 · Transparent
Parents see everything
Full risk register, evacuation plan, travel-advisory response protocol, and named medical-cover underwriter published to parents before registration opens — not after. Nothing in the operational plan is confidential to the provider.
02 · Layered
3 independent escalation paths
Student → school chaperone → Kapes trip lead → Kapes Adventures duty phone (Dubai) → Head of School. Independent medical contact via Flying Doctors Kenya direct line. No single-point-of-failure in the comms chain.
03 · Reversible
Any activity can be stood down mid-week
Any single activity can be cancelled without cascading the rest of the week. Weather, illness, or travel-advisory shift triggers the pre-signed alternate itinerary (Appendix C). The build days can compress into two if needed.
● SAFEGUARDING COMMIT
All Kapes ground staff hold current DBS-equivalent checks (Kenya DCI clearance). No 1:1 adult-child contact is permitted off-site or unsupervised. All photography of Kenyan children requires signed community consent; photography of visiting students requires parental opt-in collected at registration.

Likely questions at the table

Pre-drafted answers,
for the champion.

Q1 · Safety
"Is Kenya safe for 15-year-olds right now?"
Nairobi and the Rongai corridor are within standard FCDO "see our advice and prepare" guidance — the same risk tier as much of Southeast Asia. Our itinerary stays within that operating envelope. Coastal regions are excluded. Advisory monitored daily; trip auto-cancels on upgrade to "essential travel only". See p.10 for residuals.
Q2 · Voluntourism
"How is this not just voluntourism with a coat of paint?"
Three tests, all documented: (1) the programme runs year-round without us — six Kenyan staff on continuing payroll; (2) our students are assigned to Kenyan leads, not the reverse; (3) the infrastructure they help build stays in active service. We do not design projects for visitors. We run a programme; your cohort joins it mid-build.
Q3 · Cost
"USD 3,000 is a lot. What's actually different?"
Pricing posture is transparent in principle: leadership sees the total, the share going directly to the community build, and the non-negotiable safeguards it funds. 20% of the fee flows straight into Oloosirkon Primary School's build, bonded by payment escrow. Benchmark providers charge USD 3,800–6,000 with no public equivalent. Flights are excluded and quoted separately at booking — avoiding the margin mark-up most providers apply.
Q4 · Academics
"How is this tied to what we actually teach?"
Full IB CAS mapping on p.8 (all seven learning outcomes evidenced), optional DP Global Politics / Geography EE pathway, UAE National Agenda food-security pillar. Pre-trip modules sit in the existing Service Learning timetable slot. No new period pressure.
Q5 · Precedent
"Have other schools done this? Any references?"
The pilot cohort was a Dubai IB school's Grade 11 in Nov 2025 — written reference available. Two additional references on request: an IB school in Zurich (2024) and a UK independent (2023). Three Heads available to take a call before the senior team review if helpful.
Q6 · Optics
"What if a parent posts a photo that makes us look bad?"
Pre-trip seminar on savior framing is explicit and assessed. Social-media guidance issued to students + parents before departure. Photos of Kenyan children require community consent obtained on-site. We've built the programme to be photographable honestly — the problem doesn't arise if the work actually looks like partnership.
