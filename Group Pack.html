Executive summary · page 03 Read time: 3 minutes
In one page

The short version,
for anyone who reads no further.

  1. What Kapes is
    Dubai-based, Kenyan-led educational-travel partner running three year-round programmes in Kenya — S2E, WEW, CC. Cohorts join existing programmes; they don't initiate them.
  2. The barriers each programme addresses
    Hunger keeps children out of class — S2E turns unused school land into farms that feed pupils. Water scarcity sends girls to fetch — WEW installs tanks at partner schools. CC is different — experiential, not impact-creating: cohorts visit Wildlife Works to see Kenyan-led conservation, ethical manufacturing, and community enterprises sharing one site.
  3. What students actually do on the trip
    Seven nights in Kenya. S2E + WEW: three working days alongside the Kenyan team on the build — soil prep + planting, or tank-build + classroom refurbishment. CC: visits — wildlife, factory, community enterprises with Kenyan leads at each. Cultural exchange, safari, reflection throughout.
  4. Why this is group-defensible
    Each S2E + WEW build leaves something that continues after the cohort leaves — a working farm, a working tank. CC is experiential by design. Pricing transparent on each programme. Impact metrics published annually. Same safeguarding standard centrally underwritten — schools inherit it.
  5. What leadership is endorsing
    Kapes as preferred school trip partner, providing social impact programmes for [Group name]. Schools opt in individually; pilot in the 2026/27 academic year, group rollout from 2027/28. Group commitment: zero — parents pay; no minimum volumes; no call on the group P&L.

Safety & safeguarding posture · page 08 For Deputy Head (Safeguarding) sign-off
Before the risk register · the operating posture

The parent question
answered first.

The most common parent + leadership concern on any Kenya trip is safety. This page documents the posture; the next logs individual risks + mitigations.
● 01
Kenyan-led ground team
Trip leaders and programme partners — all Kenyan. On the ground for every trip; not flown in.
● 02
Public liability cover
PL insurance underwritten for every trip. Certificate available on request.
● 03
Flying Doctors Kenya
Medical evacuation cover active for every trip — Nairobi Hospital 45 min from base.
OPERATING POSTURE · THREE PILLARS
01 · Transparent
Parents see everything
Full risk register, evacuation plan, travel-advisory response protocol, and named medical-cover underwriter published to parents before registration opens — not after. Nothing in the operational plan is confidential to the provider.
02 · Layered
3 independent escalation paths
Student → school chaperone → Kapes trip lead → Kapes Adventures duty phone (Dubai) → Head of School. Independent medical contact via Flying Doctors Kenya direct line. No single-point-of-failure in the comms chain.
03 · Reversible
Any activity can be stood down mid-week
Any single activity can be cancelled without cascading the rest of the week. Weather, illness, or travel-advisory shift triggers the pre-signed alternate itinerary (Appendix C). The build days can compress into two if needed.
● SAFEGUARDING COMMIT
All Kapes ground staff hold current DBS-equivalent checks (Kenya DCI clearance). No 1:1 adult-child contact is permitted off-site or unsupervised. All photography of Kenyan children requires signed community consent; photography of visiting students requires parental opt-in collected at registration.

● WHY THE PRICES DIFFER
Each programme has its own accommodation, ground transport, build-day staffing, and partner-school structure. Bundling them under a single price would either over-charge cohorts going to a leaner programme or under-fund the heavier ones. A discovery call confirms cohort size, dates and programme choice; pricing follows.
● INCLUDED IN THE PER-STUDENT FEE
  • 7 nights accommodation, full board
  • All in-country ground transport + driver fees
  • Kenyan trip leaders + programme partners for full programme
  • Programme build — materials, Kenyan-team day-rates (S2E + WEW)
  • Park fees, permits, cultural + wildlife excursions
  • Public liability insurance, Flying Doctors cover, medical kit
  • Pre-trip modules + post-trip impact reporting
● NOT INCLUDED
  • Return flights — quoted separately at booking, no mark-up
  • Travel insurance — purchased direct by parents (Allianz Global Student or equivalent, mandatory)

  • Personal spending money (rec. USD 80)

Q0 · Choosing
"How do schools in the group choose between the three programmes?"
Match by cohort fit. CC — younger secondary (Y7–Y9): wildlife, conservation, ethical manufacturing as an early introduction to "Africa as it actually is". S2E — food security / sustainability / geography (Y10–Y11). WEW — gender equity / infrastructure / service learning (Y9–Y11). Schools commonly rotate across years.
Q1 · Safety
"Is Kenya safe for 15-year-olds right now?"
Nairobi and the Rongai / Voi corridors are within standard FCDO "see our advice and prepare" guidance — the same risk tier as much of Southeast Asia. Our itinerary stays within that operating envelope. Coastal regions are excluded. Advisory monitored daily; trip auto-cancels on upgrade to "essential travel only". Risk register on p.09 with named residuals.
Q2 · Voluntourism
"How is this not just voluntourism with a coat of paint?"
Three tests: (1) S2E + WEW run year-round without us — 30+ Kenyans on continuing payroll; (2) cohorts are assigned to Kenyan leads, not the reverse; (3) the build stays in active service. CC is honest about being experiential — cohorts visit Wildlife Works, they don't construct anything. We don't design projects for visitors; cohorts join programmes that already exist.
Q3 · Cost
"What's actually different about Kapes pricing?"
Each programme is priced on its own merits — S2E, WEW and CC have different durations, accommodation and ground requirements, so the per-student fees aren't identical. Pricing is shared once a programme is selected and a cohort size confirmed. Benchmark providers in this segment charge USD 3,800–6,000. Flights are excluded and quoted separately at booking.
Q4 · Academics
"How is this tied to what we actually teach?"
Whichever framework your schools use — IB (full CAS LO mapping + DP/EE pathways), British curriculum (GCSE / IGCSE / A-level), or a generic cross-curriculum service strand. Pre-trip modules sit in the existing service-learning timetable slot. Detail on p.07.
Q5 · Precedent
"Have other schools done this? Any references?"
Yes. The first Kapes Adventures trip ran in 2022 with The Arbor School (Dubai) — Brett Girvin, then principal, brought two more Arbor cohorts on Community Conservation trips in 2023 and 2024. Brett's on-record video reference is on our website. The Seeds2Education pilot ran with Fairgreen International School (Dubai) in November 2025. Both Heads available to take a call on request.
Q6 · Optics
"What if a parent posts a photo that makes us look bad?"
Pre-trip seminar on savior framing is explicit and assessed. Social-media guidance issued to students + parents before departure. Photos of Kenyan children require community consent obtained on-site. We've built the programme to be photographable honestly — the problem doesn't arise if the work actually looks like partnership.
