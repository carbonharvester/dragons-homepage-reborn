Three changes for 2026–27

Two questions and one policy line.

Each recommendation targets a dimension where you scored 1/3 or 0/3. Sequenced by what's easiest to ask first; none requires changing operator.

01

Ask your operator: "What % of our fee reaches the community?"

Fixes: Transparency · 1→3
The exact ask. Email your account lead with one question: "Of our per-student fee, what percentage is committed to community-side spend (salaries, materials, infrastructure), and can you share the audit line?" Good operators answer in one paragraph and attach a one-page breakdown. Operators that hedge, redirect, or quote a "rough range" without numbers are telling you the answer.
Low effort One email · 5 minutes Action by · Half-term break
02

Ask: "What happens at the partner site in August?"

Fixes: Site continuity · 0→3 (highest single-question lift)
The exact ask. "When our group leaves, what activity continues at the partner site — staff, programs, community use? Can you share the year-round log?" Three honest answers: (1) "Full year-round programs, here's the log" → top quartile. (2) "Other school groups visit" → tourism product in service-learning clothes. (3) "Quiet" → the site exists for visiting students, which is the textbook voluntourism pattern.
Medium effort One email + one follow-up Action by · End of term
03

Set a safeguarding floor: zero direct-to-minor activities.

Fixes: Safeguarding · 1→3 + protects the school
The exact ask. Add one line to your trip policy: "Itineraries must not include activities where students lead, teach, or have unstructured contact with local minors. School visits permitted only with full staff present and pre-cleared by the safeguarding lead." The sector bar moved five years ago — schools that moved with it stop having uncomfortable parent meetings later.

Page 05 · The framework, in plain English Kapes · Impact Scorecard · St. Catherine's College
What we're actually measuring

Three principles. Six dimensions.

The Scorecard isn't ours alone — it's an operationalisation of the past decade of academic and NGO writing on volunteer travel. We've turned principles into questions a head of department can actually answer.

PRINCIPLE 01
Continuity beats spectacle.
Sustainable impact requires partner work that runs whether students are present or not. We measure this through duration (how long you're on the ground) and site continuity (what happens between visits). The single most predictive dimension on the entire scorecard.
PRINCIPLE 02
Students join, they don't lead.
Genuine service-learning has students participating in work the community is already doing — not delivering work the community wouldn't ask for. We measure this through student roles (what they actually do day-to-day) and safeguarding (whether activities involve direct contact with minors, which is the most common voluntourism failure mode).
PRINCIPLE 03
Money behaves like it matters.
The fee structure is the truth-teller. We measure this through operator (who designs the program — schools and locally-rooted operators outperform UK/US operators with subcontractors) and transparency (whether the % to community is published, audited, and answerable). When the spend is opaque, the impact almost always is too.

Questions you'd ask if we were sitting across the table

Things we'd rather get out of the way.

01
"Is this a sales tool dressed up as a diagnostic?"
Honest answer: it serves both purposes, and we'd rather you know that than pretend otherwise. The framework is real and operator-agnostic — nothing in your recommendations requires Kapes. About 60% of schools who score the assessment never speak to us; they fix things with their existing partners. We're fine with that. The 40% who do reach out are usually the ones whose existing operator can't answer page-5's questions.
02
"Why isn't there a benchmark or network comparison?"
The assessment is brand new — we deliberately don't cite a "network average" or rank you against other schools yet. As more schools complete it we'll publish the aggregate openly, with anonymised distribution and methodology. For now this report is your diagnostic, not a leaderboard.
03
"Why does 'building something' score lower than 'joining' work?"
Because the building is rarely necessary. A decade of NGO write-ups have shown that the schoolhouse-built-by-volunteers / latrine-block-painted-by-students model produces work the community didn't ask for and often dismantles after the trip. "Joining" — students contributing to a program the community runs year-round — is the harder thing for an operator to deliver, which is why it scores higher.
04
"Our operator says they're transparent. We just haven't asked. Does that count?"
No — and we know that's harsh. Transparency that hasn't been requested is a marketing claim, not a practice. The score reflects what the school actually has visibility on, because that's what stands up at a parent or trustee meeting.
05
"We retook the assessment with different answers and got a different score. So it's gameable."
Yes. It's a self-reported diagnostic with no audit. The point isn't gotcha-grading — it's giving you and your team a shared vocabulary for the conversation. If you and your CAS coordinator score the same trip 22 points apart, that gap is the most useful thing this tool will ever produce for you.
06
"Will you contact us?"
No. We won't email you again unless you reply or fill in something on page 8. We don't run drip sequences on schools — they don't work and they irritate people we'd like to know.
